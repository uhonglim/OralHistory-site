Questions

What is oral history?

A recorded interview about someone’s own experiences. Memory and the interviewer’s questions shape the account. Photographs, documents and other interviews can help check dates and add context.

Why record in Hokkien?

It is the language the narrator is comfortable speaking. The recording preserves their phrasing and voice; the English translation makes the interview available to readers who do not speak it.

Can I contribute without old photographs?

Yes. Start with the recording. Photographs can be added later if you have permission to use them.

Can I correct or remove something from my interview?

Yes. Tell the project lead which passage, recording or photograph you want changed or removed. The team can remove material from this site, but cannot retrieve copies made by others.

Citing an interview

Use the citation on the interview page. For a particular passage, include the recording part and timestamp. Do not supply a date or name that has not been confirmed.

The current site is a project review copy. Ask Alan Lin before sharing it or reproducing an excerpt.

  2. Fong, Vanessa L. 2011. Paradise Redefined: Transnational Chinese Students and the Quest for Flexible Citizenship in the Developed World. Stanford University Press. Catalogue.
  3. Davin, Delia. 2005. “Women and Migration in Contemporary China.” China Report 41 (1): 29–38. DOI.
  4. Pieke, Frank N., Pál Nyíri, Mette Thunø, and Antonella Ceccagno. 2004. Transnational Chinese: Fujianese Migrants in Europe. Stanford University Press. Catalogue.
  5. Li, Yuanfei, Zai Liang, and Zhongshan Yue. 2020. “Migrant Children’s Schooling in Urban China.” N-IUSSP, December 7. Article.

Corrections

Give Alan Lin the interview title, recording part and timestamp, along with your correction. For a photograph, include its caption or filename.
