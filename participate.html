Interview Guide

Before the interview

Ask an older relative if they would like to talk about where they have lived, their work and their family. Allow 30–60 minutes, with breaks whenever they need one.

Explain why you are recording, who will keep the files and who will see the draft. They can decide whether anything goes online after reading the edited interview.

What to send

MaterialDetails
Original recordingKeep the phone’s original audio file. Include the date, place, language, duration and names of everyone present.
NamesYour name, your relationship to the narrator and the name they want readers to see.
Background notesTheir hometown, work, places they have lived and approximate dates. Include their birth year only if they wish to share it. Identify relatives mentioned in the recording.
Permission recordComplete the checklist below with the narrator. Record permission for each photograph separately.
Photographs, if availableA recent portrait and a few older photographs of family, homes, work or school. For each image, note who or what it shows, the place, approximate date, owner and permission to use it.
Your noteA short paragraph in your own words about why you chose this person and something you learned from the interview.

Photograph old prints flat and without glare. Keep the full image; mark any private addresses, identity numbers or other details that should be covered in the website copy. A portrait should ideally be at least 1600 pixels on its longer side.

Recording

  • Use the phone’s recording app and keep the original file.
  • Find a quiet room, silence notifications and place the phone about 30–50 cm from the narrator. Make a short test recording and listen back.
  • Use the language they are most comfortable speaking. Let them finish before asking the next question.
  • Ask for examples: “Can you remember a particular day?” or “Who was there?”
  • Check names, places and years aloud. An approximate date is fine if that is all they remember.
  • Ask permission to record at the start. Confirm any publication choices after they have seen the edited page.

You can begin with:

Today is [date]. My name is [name], and I’m interviewing my [relationship] in [place] about moving home, work and the children’s schooling. Are you willing to take part and be recorded? I’ll show you the edited interview before anything is published. You can choose whether we use your words, voice, name or photographs. You can skip any question or stop at any time.

Interview questions

Choose the questions that fit the person. Follow up on their answers rather than trying to finish the list.

  1. Where did you grow up? How did your family make a living?
  2. What did you hope to do when you were young?
  3. When did you leave school? What led to that decision?
  4. When did you first move away from home? Where did you go, and whose idea was it?
  5. Did you consider other places? Why did you choose this one?
  6. Where did you stay when you arrived? What work did you do? Did anyone help you?
  7. How did you choose the children’s schools?
  8. Did their schooling mean moving, buying a home or living apart? What was a typical day like then?
  9. What paperwork, fees or living arrangements did the school require?
  10. What did you want your children to get from school? Has your view changed?
  11. What was the hardest period? What helped?
  12. Was there somewhere you wanted to move but could not? What prevented it?
  13. Who did the paid work, housework and caring for others?
  14. Which place do you think of as home?
  15. What changed for the family after moving? Was there anything you missed?
  16. How do you feel about younger relatives studying elsewhere or abroad?

Ask before pursuing a difficult subject. If relatives remember an event differently, keep both accounts.

Permission checklist

Review the actual text, audio excerpts and photographs with the narrator. This record does not transfer ownership of their material.

  • Name to display: □ Full name □ Surname □ Pseudonym: __________
  • Photographs approved for use, listed by filename: __________
  • Material to leave out: __________
  • Narrator’s confirmation, date and method: __________
  • Project contact for corrections or removal: __________

Give the narrator a copy and the project contact’s details. They can ask for corrections or removal. Explain that the team cannot retrieve copies made by other people after publication.

Sending the files

Keep each interview in one folder. Use filenames such as WangRongXuan_Grandfather_Part1.m4a. Put photograph captions and permissions in a separate document, using the same image filenames.

Arrange a private file transfer with Alan Lin. The project’s completion target is 30 October 2026; agree on your submission date with him. Full recordings and permission records stay in the private project folder.
